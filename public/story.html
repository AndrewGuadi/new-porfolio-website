Before I wrote code, I owned stores.

I’ve been an entrepreneur since 2014. For years I owned liquor stores in Maryland. During the pandemic, sales went up. Some of the people buying from me were struggling with addiction. The better the stores did, the harder it was for me to feel good about my part in those transactions. I sold them.

I taught myself Python and JavaScript. I love the math and the logic of programming. But owning those stores left me with a question I still ask about the things I build. Who is better off when this works?

Why I started Authentic Dynamics

When I talk about looking out for the little guy, I think about my grandfather. Around Harrisburg, I’ve seen tradespeople paying every month for marketing they can’t measure and websites they don’t own. As someone who has had to make those buying decisions, that bothers me.

I founded Authentic Dynamics so a business owner could talk to the person building the thing, understand what they were paying for, and keep what they bought. An electrician should be able to ask me exactly where a service inquiry goes. It means a restaurant can keep the online ordering system its customers already know. Those decisions matter long after launch day.

What I want people to be able to make

I want a small shop with a good idea to be able to try it while the idea is still fresh. AI makes that first version faster and cheaper. The person who knows the customers still has the most interesting part of the job.

Take the smart room we built for My Smarter Home Services. A homeowner can walk around a virtual living room, dim the pendant by voice, and see what changes. The software can set the light to thirty percent. Only the person in the room gets to say whether that feels like home.

That curiosity also takes me outside client work. In L’Ordre, I’m building a city that follows the people living in it. That’s why I’m putting working projects here. If something catches your eye, open it. Change something. See whether you can make it more useful to you than it was to me.

There’s a life outside the shop.

Here in Central Pennsylvania, that includes working on Wish Upon a Food Truck, time spent in Toastmasters, and serving my neighbors in Hampden Township. In 2025 I ran a small write-in campaign as an independent and became the minority inspector of elections for Hampden 11.

Stronger communities mean stronger lives.

That’s my answer when someone asks why a small-business owner spends time on a festival or a precinct election. I live here, too.
